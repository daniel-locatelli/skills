Rafael Pastrana has three main papers on form-finding with automatic differentiation. I checked each one with a web search. He worked with Sigrid Adriaenssens's Form Finding Lab and Ryan Adams's LIPS group at Princeton.

### 1. Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation
- **Authors:** R. Pastrana, P. O. Ohlbrock, T. Oberbichler, P. D'Acunto, S. Parascho
- **Venue:** *Computer-Aided Design*, vol. 155, 2023. The arXiv preprint (2111.02607) came out in 2021.
- **What it does:** It extends the Combinatorial Equilibrium Modeling (CEM) method for pin-jointed bar structures that carry both tension and compression. Reverse-mode automatic differentiation computes exact gradients through CEM's sequential and iterative steps, so it no longer relies on numerical approximations. This makes constrained form-finding faster and more stable.
- **Also:** It adds a topological device called the "auxiliary trail" and releases an open-source Python tool (compas_cem).
- **Test cases:** A planar tensegrity, a tree canopy, a suspension bridge and a spiral staircase.

### 2. JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding
- **Authors:** R. Pastrana, D. Oktay, R. P. Adams, S. Adriaenssens
- **Venue:** ICML 2023 Workshop "Differentiable Almost Everything", arXiv 2307.12407.
- **What it does:** It is a differentiable force density method solver written in JAX. It uses sparse differentiable linear algebra and gradient-based optimization to design shells, cable nets and towers that meet target properties.
- **Test cases:** A shell fitted to a target shape and a cable net with prescribed edge lengths.
- **Code:** [github.com/arpastrana/jax_fdm](https://github.com/arpastrana/jax_fdm)

### 3. Real-time design of architectural structures with differentiable mechanics and neural networks
- **Authors:** R. Pastrana, E. Medina, I. M. de Oliveira, S. Adriaenssens, R. P. Adams
- **Venue:** ICLR 2025. The arXiv preprint (2409.02606) came out in 2024.
- **What it does:** A neural network is coupled to a differentiable force density method simulator and trained end-to-end. The network learns to map design targets directly to equilibrium shapes.
- **Results:** Its accuracy is comparable to direct optimization but it runs in real time, about 4 ms against about 5.8 s for direct optimization. It also outperforms purely neural baselines.
- **Test cases:** Masonry shells and cable-net towers. The model was integrated into 3D modeling software and used to fabricate a physical prototype.
- **Code:** [github.com/princetonlips/neural_fdm](https://github.com/princetonlips/neural_fdm)

The three papers move from automatic differentiation through an existing form-finding method (CEM), to a dedicated differentiable solver (JAX FDM), to using that solver to train neural networks.
