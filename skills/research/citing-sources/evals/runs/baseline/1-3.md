Rafael Pastrana (Princeton CREATE Lab, working with Sigrid Adriaenssens) has three main publications on form-finding with automatic differentiation:

**1. Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation**
Pastrana, Ohlbrock, Oberbichler, D'Acunto and Parascho. *Computer-Aided Design* vol. 155 (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.cad.2022.103435. Preprint arXiv:2111.02607 (2021).
- It builds on the Combinatorial Equilibrium Modeling (CEM) framework for pin-jointed bar structures in mixed tension and compression.
- Reverse-mode AD gives exact gradients through CEM's sequential, iterative steps, so gradient-based optimization can meet force and geometric constraints. Earlier approaches estimated these gradients numerically.
- It adds an "auxiliary trail" topological element, which widens the range of structures that can be form-found.
- It comes with an open-source Python tool.
- The case studies are a planar tensegrity, a tree canopy, a suspension bridge and a spiral staircase.

**2. JAX FDM: A Differentiable Solver for Inverse Form-Finding**
Pastrana, Oktay, Adams and Adriaenssens. ICML 2023 Workshop on Differentiable Almost Everything. arXiv:2307.12407.
- It implements the force density method in JAX with differentiable sparse operations. Gradient-based optimization then finds the FDM inputs that produce a target design.
- The examples are a shell matched to an arbitrary target shape and a cable net with prescribed edge lengths.
- Because it's written in JAX, it works with JAX's optimization and neural-network libraries. The code is open source at `arpastrana/jax_fdm` on GitHub.

**3. Real-time Design of Architectural Structures with Differentiable Mechanics and Neural Networks**
Pastrana, Medina, de Oliveira, Adriaenssens and Adams. ICLR 2025. arXiv:2409.02606.
- A neural network is paired with a differentiable mechanics simulator (an FDM-based decoder), so every generated design is guaranteed to be in mechanical equilibrium.
- It matches target shapes about as accurately as direct optimization, and generalizes better than fully neural models.
- It is about three orders of magnitude faster than direct optimization: roughly 4 ms per design versus about 5.8 s, according to one secondary review.
- It was tested on masonry shells and cable-net towers. The authors also connected it to Rhino3D and built a physical prototype.

These summaries come from the abstracts and indexing pages, not the full papers. He may also have a PhD dissertation or other conference papers on this topic that this search didn't turn up.
