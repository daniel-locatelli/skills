Yes. The main body of work comes from Rafael Pastrana, Sigrid Adriaenssens and colleagues at Princeton's Form Finding Lab:

- **Pastrana, Bletzinger, Oktay, Adams & Adriaenssens (2025), "Differentiable force density method for the design of lightweight structures"**, *CMAME*. This is the paper that deals with gridshells directly. One of its example problems is a high-dimensional inverse problem that planarizes the panels of a gridshell. The other examples are pneumatic membranes and masonry vault retrofits. Gradients mix automatic differentiation with hand-derived adjoint rules, and the method can optimize loads and support positions together with the force densities. The abstract reports large speedups over gradient-free methods and over plain automatic differentiation.
- **Pastrana, Oktay, Adams & Adriaenssens (2023), "JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding"**, ICML 2023 workshop ([arXiv 2307.12407](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.12407)). This is the open-source JAX library (`jax_fdm` on PyPI). Its examples are a shell matched to a target shape and a cable net with prescribed edge lengths. Neither is framed as a gridshell, but the solver works on any pin-jointed network.
- **Pastrana, Ohlbrock, Oberbichler, D'Acunto & Parascho (2023), "Constrained form-finding of tension-compression structures using automatic differentiation"**, *Computer-Aided Design*. This one uses the Combinatorial Equilibrium Modeling (CEM) framework, a relative of FDM, not FDM itself. I didn't check whether it includes a gridshell case.

From memory, and not checked in this search:

- Earlier work used gradients of the FDM equilibrium without autodiff, for example adjoint or sensitivity methods for thrust networks (Mueller's group at MIT) and FDM-based shape optimization from Ohsaki's group.
- Differentiable simulation of elastic or bending-active gridshells exists too, such as Panetta et al.'s X-shells, but it uses elastic rod models rather than FDM.

My search only reached abstracts and project pages, not the full papers. Check the gridshell details against the CMAME paper itself. The jax_fdm GitHub examples are the quickest place to see whether a gridshell setup is already there to reuse.
