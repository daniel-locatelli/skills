Here are five key references, cited from memory. I haven't looked them up in this session, so check the details before you cite them.

1. **Glymph, J., Shelden, D., Ceccato, C., Mussel, J., & Schober, H. (2004).** "A parametric strategy for free-form glass structures using quadrilateral planar facets." *Automation in Construction*, 13(2), 187–202.
   An early architectural paper on the problem. It covers translational and scale-trans surfaces that give planar quad (PQ) glass panels by construction. The work comes out of the Gehry Technologies and Schlaich Bergermann practice.

2. **Liu, Y., Pottmann, H., Wallner, J., Yang, Y.-L., & Wang, W. (2006).** "Geometric modeling with conical meshes and developable surfaces." *ACM Transactions on Graphics*, 25(3) (SIGGRAPH 2006), 681–689.
   The main PQ mesh paper. It sets up PQ meshes as discrete conjugate nets and optimizes them toward planarity. It also introduces conical meshes, which have offset properties that matter for node and beam design.

3. **Pottmann, H., Asperl, A., Hofer, M., & Kilian, A. (2007).** *Architectural Geometry.* Bentley Institute Press.
   The standard textbook for the field. It covers freeform surfaces, meshes, planarity, offsets and how they relate to fabrication.

4. **Zadravec, M., Schiftner, A., & Wallner, J. (2010).** "Designing quad-dominant meshes with planar faces." *Computer Graphics Forum*, 29(5) (SGP 2010), 1671–1679.
   Lays out PQ meshes along conjugate direction fields. This gives designers control over how the panels flow and where singularities go on a given surface.

5. **Pottmann, H., Eigensatz, M., Vaxman, A., & Wallner, J. (2015).** "Architectural geometry." *Computers & Graphics*, 47, 145–164.
   A survey of about ten years of research: PQ and conical meshes, offsets, panelization, support structures and rationalization. It's a good place to find further references.

**Also worth reading:** Liu, Xu, Wang & Wang (2011), "General planar quadrilateral mesh design using conjugate direction field," *ACM TOG* 30(6), on PQ remeshing of arbitrary surfaces. For the discrete differential geometry theory, see Bobenko & Suris (2008), *Discrete Differential Geometry: Integrable Structure* (AMS).
