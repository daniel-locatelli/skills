Here are five key references, from foundational work to recent surveys:

1. **Liu, Y., Pottmann, H., Wallner, J., Yang, Y.-L., & Wang, W. (2006).** Geometric modeling with conical meshes and developable surfaces. *ACM Transactions on Graphics*, 25(3), 681–689 (SIGGRAPH 2006). doi:10.1145/1141911.1141941
   This is the founding paper for planar quad (PQ) meshes in architecture. It links PQ meshes to discrete conjugate nets, optimizes faces to be planar, and introduces conical meshes, which have offsets at constant face distance and therefore clean multi-layer node geometry.

2. **Glymph, J., Shelden, D., Ceccato, C., Mussel, J., & Schober, H. (2004).** A parametric strategy for free-form glass structures using quadrilateral planar facets. *Automation in Construction*, 13(2), 187–202. doi:10.1016/j.autcon.2003.09.008
   This paper comes from practice (Gehry Technologies and schlaich bergermann partner). It shows how translational and scale-trans surfaces give planar quads by construction, and it underlies many built glass gridshells.

3. **Pottmann, H., Asperl, A., Hofer, M., & Kilian, A. (2007).** *Architectural Geometry*. Bentley Institute Press.
   This is the standard textbook. It covers the geometric basics behind PQ meshes, such as conjugate curve networks, translational surfaces, offsets and developables, aimed at architects.

4. **Zadravec, M., Schiftner, A., & Wallner, J. (2010).** Designing quad-dominant meshes with planar faces. *Computer Graphics Forum*, 29(5), 1671–1679 (SGP 2010). doi:10.1111/j.1467-8659.2010.01764.x
   This paper starts from an arbitrary freeform surface. It computes conjugate direction fields, which can follow design constraints, and extracts PQ-dominant meshes from them. It solves the problem of where to put the mesh lines on a given shape.

5. **Pottmann, H., Eigensatz, M., Vaxman, A., & Wallner, J. (2015).** Architectural geometry. *Computers & Graphics*, 47, 145–164. doi:10.1016/j.cag.2014.11.002
   This survey covers the field through 2015: PQ meshes, conical and circular meshes, torsion-free nodes, panelization, form-finding and fabrication-aware design. It's a good entry point into the wider literature.

**Also worth reading:** Tang, Sun, Gomes, Wallner & Pottmann (2014), "Form-finding with polyhedral meshes made simple," *ACM TOG* 33(4), which covers interactive PQ design with statics. Bouaziz et al. (2012), "Shape-Up," *Computer Graphics Forum* 31(5), gives a general projection-based planarity solver.
