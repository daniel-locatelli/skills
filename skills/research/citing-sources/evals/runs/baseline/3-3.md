Here are five key references on planar quad (PQ) meshes for freeform architecture, from early practice through the core theory to a survey:

1. **Glymph, J., Shelden, D., Ceccato, C., Mussel, J., & Schober, H. (2004).** "A parametric strategy for free-form glass structures using quadrilateral planar facets." *Automation in Construction*, 13(2), 187–202.
   This is the early paper from practice (Gehry Technologies and Schlaich Bergermann). It shows how translational and scale-trans surfaces give flat quad glass panels by construction.

2. **Liu, Y., Pottmann, H., Wallner, J., Yang, Y.-L., & Wang, W. (2006).** "Geometric modeling with conical meshes and developable surfaces." *ACM Transactions on Graphics*, 25(3) (Proc. SIGGRAPH), 681–689.
   This is the founding paper for computing PQ meshes. It turns the problem into an optimisation with a planarity constraint, links PQ meshes to conjugate curve networks, and introduces conical meshes, which allow clean offsets for multi-layer structures.

3. **Pottmann, H., Asperl, A., Hofer, M., & Kilian, A. (2007).** *Architectural Geometry*. Bentley Institute Press.
   This is the standard textbook for the field. It covers translational surfaces, conjugate nets, PQ meshes and panelisation, written for architects and engineers.

4. **Zadravec, M., Schiftner, A., & Wallner, J. (2010).** "Designing quad-dominant meshes with planar faces." *Computer Graphics Forum*, 29(5) (Proc. SGP), 1671–1679.
   This paper solves the layout problem: it fits conjugate direction fields on a given surface. Those fields set the mesh layout that makes nearly planar quads possible before any optimisation starts.

5. **Pottmann, H., Eigensatz, M., Vaxman, A., & Wallner, J. (2015).** "Architectural geometry." *Computers & Graphics*, 47, 145–164.
   This survey covers PQ meshes, offset meshes, support structures and panelisation, with a large bibliography for further reading.

**Next in line:** Tang, Sun, Gomes, Wallner & Pottmann (2014), "Form-finding with polyhedral meshes made simple," *ACM TOG* 33(4), on PQ meshes that are also in static equilibrium. Bouaziz et al. (2012), "Shape-Up," *CGF* 31(5), is a widely used projection-based solver for planarity constraints.
