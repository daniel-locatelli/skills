The classic reference is **Kennedy, M. C. & O'Hagan, A. (2001). "Bayesian calibration of computer models." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B*, 63(3), 425–464.** DOI: 10.1111/1467-9868.00294

It set up the standard framework, often called the "KOH" model:

- **Observations = simulator(x, θ) + model discrepancy δ(x) + observation error**
- It puts Gaussian process priors on both the simulator, which acts as an emulator for expensive codes, and the discrepancy term.
- It infers the calibration parameters θ while accounting for code uncertainty, model inadequacy and measurement noise.

**Common follow-ups:**
- **Higdon, Kennedy, Cavendish, Cafeo & Ryne (2004)**, *SIAM J. Sci. Comput.* 26(2): a fully Bayesian MCMC version of the approach.
- **Higdon, Gattiker, Williams & Rightley (2008)**, *JASA* 103(482): extends it to high-dimensional (functional) outputs using basis representations.
- **Brynjarsdóttir & O'Hagan (2014)**, *Inverse Problems* 30(11): "Learning about physical parameters: the importance of model discrepancy." This is the key paper on the identifiability problem between θ and δ.
- **Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn (1989)**, *Statistical Science* 4(4): the earlier foundation for GP emulation of computer experiments.
