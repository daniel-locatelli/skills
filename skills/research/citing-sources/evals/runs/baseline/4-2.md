The classic reference is:

**Kennedy, M. C., & O'Hagan, A. (2001).** *Bayesian calibration of computer models.* Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology), 63(3), 425–464. doi:10.1111/1467-9868.00294

It sets up what is now called the **Kennedy–O'Hagan (KOH) framework**:

- **Observation model:** field data = simulator output at the true calibration parameters θ + **model discrepancy** δ(x) + measurement error.
- **Emulation:** a Gaussian process stands in for the expensive simulator, and a second GP models the discrepancy.
- **Inference:** calibration parameters, discrepancy and emulator uncertainty are estimated together in a Bayesian way, so predictions carry all of these uncertainty sources.

**Related reading:**
- **Higdon, Kennedy, Cavendish, Cafeo & Ryne (2004),** *SIAM J. Sci. Comput.* 26(2), 448–466. A fully Bayesian (MCMC) implementation of the KOH framework.
- **Higdon, Gattiker, Williams & Rightley (2008),** *JASA* 103(482), 570–583. Extends the framework to high-dimensional (functional) outputs using basis representations.
- **Brynjarsdóttir & O'Hagan (2014),** *Inverse Problems* 30(11), 114007. "Learning about physical parameters: the importance of model discrepancy." This is the standard citation for the identifiability problem between θ and δ.
- **Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn (1989),** *Statistical Science* 4(4), 409–423. The earlier foundational paper on GP emulators (design and analysis of computer experiments).
