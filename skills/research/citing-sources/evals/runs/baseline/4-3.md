The classic reference is:

**Kennedy, M. C., & O'Hagan, A. (2001). Bayesian calibration of computer models. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)*, 63(3), 425–464.** DOI: [10.1111/1467-9868.00294](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294)

It set up the "KOH" framework that most later work builds on:

- **Model:** field observations = simulator output at the true calibration parameters + a **model discrepancy** term + measurement error.
- **Gaussian process emulator:** a GP stands in for the expensive simulator.
- **Second GP for discrepancy:** a separate GP represents the model inadequacy, so calibration accounts for the simulator being imperfect.

Related papers often cited alongside it:

- **Higdon et al. (2004, 2008):** practical extensions, including high-dimensional and functional outputs (*SIAM J. Sci. Comput.*; *JASA* 2008).
- **Bayarri et al. (2007):** "A Framework for Validation of Computer Models," *Technometrics*.
- **Brynjarsdóttir & O'Hagan (2014):** "Learning about physical parameters: the importance of model discrepancy," *Inverse Problems*. It covers the identifiability problem between calibration parameters and discrepancy.
- **Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn (1989):** "Design and Analysis of Computer Experiments," *Statistical Science*. This is the earlier foundation for GP emulation of computer codes.
