These are the papers that seem to have been mixed together:

1. **JAX FDM's journal paper is in *Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering* (CMAME), not *Automation in Construction*.** It is "Differentiable force density method for the design of lightweight structures" by Pastrana, Oktay, Bletzinger, Adams and Adriaenssens. The repo lists it as vol. 458, art. 118783, 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cma.2026.118783. The lab's citation says 2025, so the year needs checking. The paper:
   - derives the force density method from energy principles and extends it to nonlinear 3D cases with loads that change with the shape;
   - gets gradients from automatic differentiation in JAX, combined with hand-derived adjoint rules;
   - optimises force densities, loads and support positions together, which finds better designs than optimising force densities alone;
   - demonstrates shape fitting, controlling cable lengths in pneumatic structures, making gridshell panels planar while reducing support reactions, and post-tensioning to retrofit masonry;
   - checks results against reference solutions, measures CPU/GPU scaling, and includes a minimal implementation of about 50 lines of Python.
2. **Before that, JAX FDM appeared as a 2023 ICML workshop paper:** "JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding" (Differentiable Almost Everything workshop; arXiv 2307.12407).
3. **Pastrana's earlier work on automatic differentiation in form-finding is in *Computer-Aided Design*, 2023:** "Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation", vol. 155, 103435, doi:10.1016/j.cad.2022.103435. It is about the Combinatorial Equilibrium Modeling (CEM) method, not the force density method.
4. **His only *Automation in Construction* paper I found isn't about form-finding:** Bruun, Pastrana et al., 2021, on building a masonry arch without scaffolding using cooperating robots. It's in your librarian as id 22169.
