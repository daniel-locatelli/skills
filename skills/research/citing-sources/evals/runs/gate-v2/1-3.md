Rafael Pastrana has published three papers that directly use automatic differentiation (AD) for form-finding:

1. **Pastrana, R., Ohlbrock, P. O., Oberbichler, T., D'Acunto, P. & Parascho, S. (2023).** *Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation.* Computer-Aided Design 155, 103435. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2022.103435
   This is the main paper on the topic. It uses AD to solve constrained form-finding for structures that carry both tension and compression.

2. **Pastrana, R., Oktay, D., Adams, R. P. & Adriaenssens, S. (2023).** *JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding.* ICML 2023 workshop "Differentiable Almost Everything". https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12407
   This presents JAX FDM, a differentiable force density method solver written in JAX, used for inverse form-finding.

3. **Pastrana, R., Medina, E., Oliveira, I. M. de, Adriaenssens, S. & Adams, R. P. (2024).** *Real-time design of architectural structures with differentiable mechanics and neural networks.* arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02606
   This combines differentiable mechanics with neural networks to design structures in real time.

A related paper does form-finding with graph neural networks rather than AD. Pastrana is second author:

- **Bleker, L., Pastrana, R., Ohlbrock, P. O. & D'Acunto, P. (2023).** *Structural Form-Finding Enhanced by Graph Neural Networks.* In *Towards Radical Regeneration*, pp. 24–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-13249-0_3

Papers 1 and 2 are easy to confuse: both are 2023 papers by Pastrana et al. on differentiable form-finding. The CAD paper is a journal article. JAX FDM is a workshop paper with different co-authors and has no DOI. Cite each by its own link.
