Yes. The closest match is a 2026 paper from Princeton's Form Finding Lab:

- **Pastrana, Oktay, Bletzinger, Adams & Adriaenssens (2026), "Differentiable force density method for the design of lightweight structures"**, [*CMAME* 458, 118783](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118783). It makes the force density method differentiable, using automatic differentiation plus analytical adjoints. The authors report it runs up to 10⁴× faster than gradient-free methods and about 10× faster than plain automatic differentiation. According to the [Princeton listing](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/differentiable-force-density-method-for-the-design-of-lightweight/) and the [lab page](https://formfindinglab.princeton.edu/?p=3711), one of its examples is a **gridshell**: making the panels planar while minimising reaction forces. I only saw that in the abstract-level summaries, not the full text.

Earlier work by the same group:

- **Pastrana, Oktay, Adams & Adriaenssens (2023), "JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding"**, [ICML 2023 Differentiable Almost Everything workshop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12407). This is the open-source solver behind the paper above ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/jax-fdm)).
- **Pastrana, Ohlbrock, Oberbichler, D'Acunto & Parascho (2023), "Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation"**, [*CAD* 155, 103435](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2022.103435). It uses automatic differentiation with the Combinatorial Equilibrium Modeling method rather than the force density method. Don't mix this one up with JAX FDM, which came out the same year.
- **Pastrana, Medina, Oliveira, Adriaenssens & Adams (2024), "Real-time design of architectural structures with differentiable mechanics and neural networks"**, [arXiv:2409.02606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02606). It puts a differentiable mechanics solver inside a neural-network design loop.

I only ran one web search and looked up this group's papers, so this isn't a full literature review. Other groups' differentiable force density work aimed specifically at gridshells could exist that I didn't find.
