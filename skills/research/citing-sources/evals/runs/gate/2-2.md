Yes. One paper applies a differentiable force density method directly to gridshells, and a few more cover differentiable FDM in general.

**Applies it to gridshells**
- Pastrana et al. (2026, [CMAME 458, 118783](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118783)), *Differentiable force density method for the design of lightweight structures*. According to its abstract, it combines automatic differentiation with analytical adjoints to solve large inverse problems. One of its examples is making gridshell panels planar; the others are pneumatic membranes and retrofitting masonry vaults. It reports large speedups over gradient-free methods and over plain automatic differentiation.

**Earlier differentiable FDM (not gridshell-specific)**
- Pastrana et al. (2023, [CAD 155](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2022.103435)), *Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation*.
- Pastrana, Oktay, Adams & Adriaenssens (2023), *JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding*, from an ICML 2023 workshop ([arXiv:2307.12407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12407)). This is the open-source solver, available as [`jax-fdm` on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/jax-fdm).
- Pastrana et al. (2024), *Real-time design of architectural structures with differentiable mechanics and neural networks* ([arXiv:2409.02606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02606)).

**Related**
- Cuvilliers (2020), *The constrained geometry of structures: optimization methods for inverse form-finding design*, an MIT PhD thesis ([OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/W3118317158), [DSpace@MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/127853)). It covers gradient-based inverse form-finding and compares general-purpose optimizers with domain-specific ones.
- The search also found a 2026 paper from Politecnico di Milano in *Engineering Structures* that extends FDM to gridshells that resist bending in the vertical plane ([PDF](https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/4eaf9ba0-96cf-4c13-93e5-1da2823f7163/1-s2.0-S0141029626002907-main.pdf)). I didn't check its record, so I haven't confirmed the authors or title. It's the closest work on bending, but the search summary doesn't say it uses automatic differentiation.

**Possible gap:** I didn't find a paper that combines a differentiable FDM with bending-active or bending-resistant gridshells. The Pastrana 2026 paper only uses gridshells for the panel-planarity example. This was a quick web and OpenAlex search, not a full literature review.
