Here are five key references, from the first built projects to the standard optimisation tools:

1. **Glymph, J., Shelden, D., Ceccato, C., Mussel, J. & Schober, H. (2004).** A parametric strategy for free-form glass structures using quadrilateral planar facets. *Automation in Construction* 13, 187–202. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2003.09.008
   This is where the problem comes from in practice (Gehry Partners with schlaich bergermann). It builds flat quad glass panels by translating or scaling one curve along another, so every face is planar by construction.

2. **Liu, Y., Pottmann, H., Wallner, J., Yang, Y. L. & Wang, W. (2006).** Geometric modeling with conical meshes and developable surfaces. *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 25, 681–689. https://doi.org/10.1145/1141911.1141941
   This is the foundational paper. It turns planar-quad (PQ) meshes into an optimisation problem, links them to conjugate curve networks, and introduces conical meshes, which have offset meshes at constant face distance. That is what makes multi-layer glass and steel construction possible.

3. **Liu, Y., Xu, W., Wang, J., Zhu, L., Guo, B., Chen, F. & Wang, G. (2011).** General planar quadrilateral mesh design using conjugate direction field. *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 30, 1–10. https://doi.org/10.1145/2070781.2024174
   Computes conjugate direction fields with singularities on an arbitrary surface. This lets you lay out a PQ mesh on any freeform surface, not only on surfaces that already have a convenient grid of curves.

4. **Bouaziz, S., Deuss, M., Schwartzburg, Y., Weise, T. & Pauly, M. (2012).** Shape‐Up: Shaping Discrete Geometry with Projections. *Computer Graphics Forum* 31, 1657–1667. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03171.x
   A general solver that alternates between projecting each element onto its constraint and blending the results. Making faces planar is the standard use case, and the method underlies many interactive planarisation tools, Kangaroo-style.

5. **Pottmann, H., Eigensatz, M., Vaxman, A. & Wallner, J. (2015).** Architectural geometry. *Computers & Graphics* 47, 145–164. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cag.2014.11.002
   A survey of the field: PQ meshes, conical and circular meshes, offsets, support structures, and how panelling relates to cost. It's a good entry point into the wider literature.
