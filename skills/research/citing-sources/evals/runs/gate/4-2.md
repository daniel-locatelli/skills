The classic reference is the "KOH" paper:

Kennedy, M. C. & O'Hagan, A. (2001). Bayesian Calibration of Computer Models. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology* 63, 425-464. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294

It introduced the framework most later work builds on. Field observations are modelled as the simulator's output at the best-fitting values of its unknown inputs, plus a "model discrepancy" term for the gap between the model and reality, plus measurement error. The simulator and the discrepancy are each given a Gaussian process prior. That lets the method account for uncertainty in the parameters, in the model's structure and in the expensive simulator's output, all at once.

Don't confuse it with the same authors' 2002 Springer chapter, "Bayesian Analysis of Computer Code Outputs" (https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4471-0657-9_11, with N. A. Higgins). That chapter is a different and much less cited work.
