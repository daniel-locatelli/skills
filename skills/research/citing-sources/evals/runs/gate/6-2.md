My lookup tool's own notes record this exact mistake: two real papers getting merged into a "JAX FDM, *Automation in Construction*" reference that was never published. The JAX FDM paper itself is a 2023 workshop paper at ICML (a machine-learning conference) and is on arXiv:

**Pastrana, R., Oktay, D., Adams, R. P. & Adriaenssens, S. (2023). JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding.** ICML 2023, Differentiable Almost Everything Workshop. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12407

## Summary (from the arXiv abstract)

- **What it is:** JAX FDM is a differentiable solver for designing mechanically efficient 3D structures, such as domes, cable nets and towers. The resulting shapes are made to meet target architectural, fabrication and structural requirements.
- **Problem:** *Inverse* form-finding. Instead of computing the shape that a given set of forces produces, it searches for the forces that produce a shape meeting the targets.
- **Method:** It combines three things:
  - the **force density method** (FDM);
  - **differentiable sparsity**, which keeps the sparse equilibrium solve differentiable;
  - **gradient-based optimisation**.
- **Integration:** It is built on JAX (a Python library that computes gradients automatically), so it can be combined with other JAX libraries. One example is putting form-finding simulations inside neural-network pipelines.
- **Results:** The paper shows two design examples. The library is open source at https://github.com/arpastrana/jax_fdm.
