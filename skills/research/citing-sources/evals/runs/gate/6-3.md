The skill I use for looking up citations warns about exactly this name: it is a known mix-up of two real papers.

- **JAX FDM**: Pastrana, R., Oktay, D., Adams, R. P. & Adriaenssens, S. (2023). *JAX FDM: A differentiable solver for inverse form-finding*. ICML 2023 Differentiable Almost Everything Workshop. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12407
- **The journal paper it gets confused with**: Pastrana et al. (2022, [CAD 155](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2022.103435)), *Constrained Form-Finding of Tension–Compression Structures using Automatic Differentiation*. It is in *Computer-Aided Design*, not *Automation in Construction*.

**Summary of the JAX FDM paper.** This is from my general knowledge of the paper. I only pulled its citation details, not the full text, so check specifics against the arXiv PDF.

- **Problem:** Inverse form-finding means finding the internal forces that make a structure take a shape you want. Examples are cable nets, tensile membranes and funicular shells and vaults. Usually this is done by trial and error, or with solvers built for one kind of target.
- **Method:** The paper rewrites the force density method (FDM) in JAX, a Python library that can compute gradients automatically, so the whole equilibrium calculation is differentiable. The force densities become design variables. A gradient-based optimiser then tunes them to reduce a goal you define, such as matching a target shape, limiting edge lengths or forces, or reducing load path, while the structure stays in equilibrium.
- **Contributions:** An open-source, flexible solver with gradients computed automatically rather than derived by hand, so you can combine goals and constraints freely. It also runs fast on hardware accelerators (JIT compilation).
- **Demonstrations:** Several form-finding examples for shape-forming structures. Because the solver is written in JAX, it also connects to machine-learning workflows.
